## 2. Employee (HR) Dashboard

### Objective

Give HR a single view of workforce size, attrition and employee experience.

### What I built

- KPI cards: **Total Employees, Current Employees, Terminated, Termination Rate, Total Salary**
- **Quarterly New Hires** and **Quarterly Terminations**
- **Monthly Salary Trends** and **Monthly Termination Trends**
- **Current Employees by Gender** (donut chart)
- **Employee Education**, **Work-Life Balance** and **Job Satisfaction** breakdowns
- Scatter plots comparing **years at company / total working years vs salary** by employment type and gender

### Headline numbers

| Metric               | Value      |
| -------------------- | ---------- |
| Total Employees      | 1,470      |
| Current Employees    | 1,233      |
| Terminated           | 237        |
| **Termination Rate** | **16.12%** |

### Key insights

- **Attrition is significant:** about 1 in 6 employees (16.12%) has left.
- **Hiring is steady,** at roughly 350-385 new hires every quarter, with no strong seasonality.
- **Terminations were higher early in the year** (about 28 per month in January-February) and then settled near 20 per month.
- **Workforce is male-majority:** 732 men (59.4%) vs 501 women (40.6%) among current employees.
- **Education:** Bachelor's degree is the most common level, followed by Master's; Doctorate holders are the smallest group.
- **Work-life balance:** 60.75% rate it "Better" and about 10% "Best"; only a small share rate it "Bad".
- **Job satisfaction:** about 61% report High or Very High satisfaction, while about 39% report Low or Medium. That is a meaningful group to watch for retention.

### Business recommendations

- Break attrition down by **department, tenure, salary band and satisfaction** to find the real drivers.
- Focus retention efforts on the Low/Medium satisfaction groups and on the early-year attrition spike.
- Review gender balance in hiring pipelines.
